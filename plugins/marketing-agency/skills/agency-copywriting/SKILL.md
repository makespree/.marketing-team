# Creative copy

Read product context, brand voice, format and the current brief. Establish audience,
single message, awareness level, desired response, proof and permitted brand presence.

Prefer clarity, specificity, active language and customer vocabulary. Connect a
feature to a useful outcome without inventing performance numbers. The source
library's illustrative numbers are not facts about this product. Don't force a
CTA onto an artwork intended simply to create affinity.

For campaigns: identify a human observation, creative mechanism and payoff.
For websites: headline → value/proof → how it works → objections → next action.
For reels: hook → development → payoff, with copy timed for actual reading.
For carousels: opening promise → escalating usefulness/reveal → satisfying close.
For captions: add meaning or context instead of restating every visual.
Adapt these patterns when the brief demands a different structure.

Use natural language appropriate to the audience, not canned superlatives,
corporate filler, generated-sounding slogans or obligatory rhetorical questions.
Humour must fit the brand and situation. Style references guide craft, not impersonation.
Facts, testimonials, price/offer terms and scarcity need evidence.
Do not claim a phrase is algorithm-safe or guarantees reach without current support.

Return ready-to-use copy. Offer alternate hooks when a decision is genuinely open,
with a concise explanation of the difference. Label draft claims or placeholders.
Save exact approved copy alongside its creative revision; generation/layout must
not silently alter it. Review spelling and meaning on the final export too.
