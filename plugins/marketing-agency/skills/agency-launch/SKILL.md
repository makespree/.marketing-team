# Launch strategy

Read product context, real readiness, audience, resources and existing authorizations.
Plan for the product type: a consumer brand, creative IP, local service and B2B SaaS
need different launches. Do not default to Product Hunt, a waitlist or artificial scarcity.

Define the audience, changed experience, one promise, proof, primary action and
objection. Use the Jobs framing lens for a clear reveal and the Dunford lens for
differentiation. A dramatic reveal cannot rescue an untrue claim.

Choose a small set of channels:
- Owned: website, email list or community where there is actual access.
- Rented: social platforms or marketplaces relevant to this audience.
- Borrowed: partnerships, press, creators or guest appearances with a credible fit.
An owned channel still depends on audience consent and delivery infrastructure.
A partnership idea is a proposed contact, not a sent pitch.

Sequence preparation, release, follow-through and learning. Every phase names:
audience, message, creative deliverable, destination, owner, dependency, decision
date if known, and success measure. Include product onboarding and next action,
not just a launch-day spike. Size effort to the available team and budget.

Readiness: verify the promised path works, CTA destination exists, proof is approved,
assets and tracking are ready, and responsibility for incoming demand is clear.
A missing nonessential feature need not delay the launch; a broken core promise does.

Return a compact positioning/reveal plan, asset slate, channel sequence, readiness
gaps and experiment definition. Create samples only within the requested stage.
Publish/schedule/contact/spend through separately authorized project capabilities.
