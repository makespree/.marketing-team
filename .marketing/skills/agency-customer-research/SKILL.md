# Customer and cultural research

Read project context and the job's actual question. Choose among supplied evidence,
public research, or an authorized interview/survey. Do not contact anyone merely
because outreach templates or connectors exist.

For transcripts, reviews, support tickets and public discussions, extract:
job to be done; functional/emotional/social needs; pain; trigger; desired outcome;
alternatives; objections; exact language. Keep source URL/file, date, excerpt,
context, segment and whether prompted. Observe applicable privacy and quotation limits.
Don't infer sensitive demographics to fabricate a target persona.

Group themes, retain contradictions and look for disconfirmation. Explain confidence
using sample quality, independence, recency and relevance, not a magic source count.
A handful of comments can suggest a test; they cannot prove what all buyers want.
Separate direct customers from proxy audiences and promotional claims.

For cultural research, inspect rituals, humour, craft, references and ordinary
situations in the audience's world. Avoid reducing a region to decorative stereotypes.
Reference popularity is not proven effectiveness. Record any observed metric with
date and denominator; avoid causal explanations unsupported by evidence.

Return the evidence most useful to the decision: source ledger, audience tensions,
quote bank, provisional personas if warranted, unanswered questions and implications.
Use .marketing/vault/research/ or the active job; do not create a second context store.
Use the Teresa Torres lens for observation and Piyush Pandey lens for cultural
interpretation, without attributing your own conclusions to them.
