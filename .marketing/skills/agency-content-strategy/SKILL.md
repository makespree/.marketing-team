# Content strategy

Read product truth, audience research, brand and previous results. Frame the goal
before choosing volume or formats. Choose among searchable, shareable, educational,
cultural, entertaining and action-oriented content according to that goal.
SEO is not a universal first priority.

Explore the audience's wider world: craft, rituals, identity, nostalgia, uncertainty,
aspiration, utility, relationships and unexpected facts. A product need not appear
in every piece. Generate distinct territories rather than minor versions of one joke.

For each shortlisted idea record:
- specific observation and evidence;
- who cares, and why now;
- one message and creative mechanism;
- likely response (recognition, send, save, discussion, visit or purchase);
- format, reference roles, assets and production effort;
- product/brand relationship and next action if appropriate;
- how it will be evaluated and what remains uncertain.

Separate the subject from the mechanism. A food guide, for example, may become
useful through selection and shareable through a recognizable social situation.
Transfer that reasoning to this product rather than copying the example topic.
Do not invent reach estimates or reuse expert/research numbers as forecasts.

Prioritize on audience relevance, distinctiveness, proof, objective fit and effort.
Use scores only when they help a real choice; explain assumptions. Propose a
manageable slate with deliberate variety. No mandatory cadence, 60/30/10 split
or indiscriminate posting across all channels.

Design a coherent campaign grammar; make new art when the new concept needs it.
Repurpose strong work where the channel fits, with readable crop/copy adjustments.
Use actual results to revise the next slate. Without results, label it a hypothesis.
