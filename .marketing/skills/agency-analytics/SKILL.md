# Measurement and learning

Start from the decision: what result would make the team keep, change or stop a
creative approach? Read project context and existing measurement before proposing
new tracking. A marketing brief does not authorize product instrumentation changes.

Define objective, event/metric, source, time window, denominator, baseline and
comparison. Distinguish impressions, unique reach, engagement, clicks, qualified
visits, activated users, purchases and revenue. Missing data is unknown, not zero.
Record account timezone, currency, attribution window and exclusions when relevant.

For a test, state hypothesis, audience, creative variable, allocation, primary metric,
guardrails, duration/decision rule and confounders. Different audiences or spend
can invalidate a creative-only comparison. Small observations justify another test,
not certainty. Do not declare a winner from noisy or incomplete figures.

For setup requests, draft an event plan before implementation: name, trigger,
properties, owner, consent requirements and verification. Check current product and
platform documentation; do not embed stale legal rules or provider syntax as timeless.
Avoid personal data in analytics. Verify real event behaviour if implementation
is authorized; a source-code hook alone is not evidence of collected events.

For results requests, read actual exports or authorized connectors. Retain source
dates and sanitized receipts. Don't sum unique reach across overlapping audiences.
Separate brand effects over time from immediate response using the Binet/Field lens;
there is no universal budget ratio or automatic spend increase.

Return observed facts, uncertainty, interpretation and the next concrete creative
brief. Keep campaign and creative revision identifiers so learning maps to the
actual content. Paid actions and account changes require their applicable scope.
